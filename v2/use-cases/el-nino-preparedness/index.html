    El Niño season preparedness¶

    A national water department wants to turn a seasonal climate signal into a water-resources decision. This page follows the application case that the Department of Water and Sanitation (DWS) of Botswana prepared for IWMI in October 2026, when it tested the portal as a water manager, not as a climate viewer, ahead of the 2026/27 El Niño season.

    The question¶

    El Niño says pay attention, but not where, when or how water resources will respond. Rainfall, soil moisture and river-flow forecasts say whether the climate concern is becoming a water-resources problem. The useful endpoint is lead time: time to verify, communicate, review allocations and prepare.

    The test question was therefore: can the portal support that whole chain, from climate signal to management action, for one country during one season?

    Who Decision Cadence
    National water department (DWS Botswana) Verification, communication, allocation review, preparedness Weekly situation brief through the season
    Dam and water-supply operators Hold or release storage; supply restrictions Each forecast run during a watch
    Basin committee / LIMCOM Cross-border allocation and early-warning coordination Seasonal (Oct, Jan, Apr)
    Disaster-management authorities Pre-positioning for short-duration high-flow events When a gauge crosses a threshold

    At a glance — what the October 2026 test found

    • The strongest drought workflow is rainfall → root-zone soil moisture: did rainfall depart from normal, and did that reach the catchment?
    • A seasonal drought concern can coexist with a short-duration high-flow event. Gauge A5H003 (Limpopo at the Botswana border) forecast 90.5 m³/s at 72 h, above its P90 minor threshold of 71.05 m³/s, while the 30-day outlook was back to normal.
    • The portal's practical contribution is lead time: multiple forecast horizons against station thresholds, in one basin view that crosses national boundaries.
    • Keep both watches open all season: drought (deficits, soil moisture, low flow, storage) and high flow (short-term forecasts).

    How the workflow fits together¶

    flowchart TD
    A[1 · Rainfall<br/>CHIRPS · Rainfall Anomalies] --> AQ{Is the seasonal<br/>signal appearing?}
    AQ -- no --> Z[Routine weekly brief]
    AQ -- yes --> B[2 · Soil moisture<br/>SMAP Root Zone Soil Moisture]
    B --> BQ{Is catchment stress<br/>developing?}
    BQ -- yes --> C[3 · River flow<br/>GloFAS Verified Gauges · Eflow Alert Levels]
    BQ -- no --> Z
    C --> D[4 · Forecast<br/>alert level per lead time vs P90 / P95 / P99]
    D --> E[5 · Action<br/>verify · communicate · allocate · prepare]

    The test used only what was visible in the live portal. Where a layer did not render or a value could not be verified, that was recorded as a user-experience finding rather than filled with assumptions (see Limits).

    Data you will use¶

    Stage Dataset Where in the catalogue Notes
    Rainfall CHIRPS Historical Rainfall (display variable Rain Status) · Rainfall Anomalies Drought Monitoring & Risk Assessment → Meteorological Drought Station circles; anomalies against the 1991–2020 normal, clickable for a chart
    Soil SMAP Root Zone Soil Moisture (L4, Daily) → Soil Moisture ~9 km, daily; the root zone is what crops and catchments feel
    River flow and forecast GloFAS Discharge Forecast – Verified Gauges (display variable Forecast 720h M3s) Flood Monitoring → Discharge Forecasting (GloFAS) Click a gauge for the Flood Intelligence card: 24 h / 72 h / 30-day forecasts, P90 / P95 / P99 thresholds, hydrograph
    Rivers (drought side) E-flow Monitoring and Forecasting · Eflow Alert Levels → Hydrological Drought & E-flows Low-flow confirmation
    Storage Dams & Reservoirs Basin Level Estimates Water Resources → Reservoirs Storage class per dam
    Seasonal outlook Limpopo Rainfall Anomaly Forecast · Seasonal Forecast Anomaly (7-month) Natural Basin Characteristic → Climate Probabilistic anomalies

    Walkthrough¶

    1. Is the seasonal signal appearing? Explore data → search CHIRPS → add CHIRPS Historical Rainfall and set the display variable to Rain Status; step the timeline through the first months of the season (Figure 1). Then add Rainfall Anomalies and click the stations in your area for the departure from the 1991–2020 normal.
    2. Is catchment stress developing? Add SMAP Root Zone Soil Moisture (L4, Daily) (Figure 2). A rainfall deficit that shows up in the root zone has reached the catchment; one that does not is still a weather story.
    3. How is the system responding? Add GloFAS Discharge Forecast – Verified Gauges, set the display variable to Forecast 720h M3s and click a gauge, for Botswana A5H003 – Limpopo River @ Botswana. The card gives the 24 h, 72 h and 30-day forecasts, the alert level of each, and the station's thresholds.
    4. What may happen next? Open the Flood Risk tab for the alert level at every lead time (Figure 3), then the Hydrograph tab for the shape and spread of the forecast (Figure 4). Use the lead time that matches the management question: days for operations, weeks for allocation.
    5. What should the department do? Translate the levels into the Green / Amber / Red actions agreed for that gauge (see Indicators and what to do).
    6. Keep the drought watch open. In the same session add Eflow Alert Levels and Dams & Reservoirs Basin Level Estimates: low flows and storage pressure are the slow signal behind the fast one.
    7. Save the sequence as a Story: one scene per stage is the weekly brief.

    Reading the charts¶

    The figures below are the portal's own screens from the October 2026 test.

    • Judge the pattern, not one station: a season that starts light across a whole sub-basin while the normal is moderate is the first El Niño signature.
    • This pairing, rainfall deficit over dry root zone, was the strongest drought workflow in the test: it separates it has not rained from the catchment is under stress.
    • Read the path, not one number: Normal → Watch → Minor (72–120 h) → Watch → Normal is a short-duration episode with a few days of lead time, not a seasonal flood.
    • The 96 h peak approaches but stays below the P95 major threshold: a verification-and-prepare situation, not an evacuation one.
    • The rise is rapid over the next few days and the spread widens with lead time; the longer lead times return toward normal. Management value: time to verify against the department's own gauges and to prepare.

    Indicators and what to do¶

    The portal gives the levels; the department sets the actions. The test proposed Botswana-calibrated Green / Amber / Red thresholds per gauge, tied to actions. A starting point consistent with the portal's own classes:

    Watch Indicator Level Typical action
    High flow GloFAS gauge forecast Normal at all lead times Routine weekly brief
    High flow GloFAS gauge forecast Watch (above P90 at any lead time) Verify with national gauges; alert operators
    High flow GloFAS gauge forecast Minor (P90–P95 within 5 days) Communicate downstream; review dam operations
    High flow GloFAS gauge forecast Major or Extreme (above P95 / P99) Disaster-management protocol (see Floods)
    Drought Rainfall Anomalies Below normal for 2+ consecutive months in Nov–Mar Allocation review; drought advisory
    Drought SMAP root-zone moisture Below seasonal norm for > 4 weeks Irrigation scheduling advice; livestock water points
    Drought Eflow Alert Levels · reservoir class Level 3+ or Low storage Abstraction limits; supply restrictions (see Droughts)

    Worked example — the October 2026 test at A5H003¶

    1. Add GloFAS Discharge Forecast – Verified Gauges, display variable Forecast 720h M3s, and click A5H003 – Limpopo River @ Botswana. The card reads: 24 h 21.8 m³/s (Normal), 72 h 90.5 m³/s (Minor), 30-day 10.7 m³/s (Normal).
    2. Flood Risk tab (Figure 3): the level climbs through Watch at 48 h to Minor at 72, 96 and 120 h, peaks at 104.6 m³/s, and falls back to Normal by 240 h. Against the station's P95 major threshold of 108.87 m³/s this is a short-duration high-flow episode.
    3. Hydrograph tab (Figure 4): the rise is steep and the spread band wide, so verify before acting: compare with the department's own readings for the same reach.
    4. Meanwhile the seasonal picture (Figures 1 and 2) is of a dry start with a drying root zone: the drought watch stays open while the high-flow watch runs its few days.
    5. The decision: Amber for the reach for 3–5 days (verify, inform operators downstream), with the seasonal drought narrative unchanged. Captured as a Story, that is the week's brief.

    Briefing checklist¶

    The test proposed a weekly El Niño water-resources situation brief. Its contents, in the order of the workflow:

    • Rainfall: season-to-date status and anomaly at the national stations
    • Root-zone soil moisture relative to the seasonal norm
    • Observed and forecast flow at the agreed priority gauges, with alert level per lead time
    • Reservoir storage class and groundwater trend
    • Risk statement, confidence, and the recommended Green / Amber / Red action
    • Story link reproducing the scenes

    Limits to keep in mind¶

    Findings reported by the test, for the portal team as much as for users:

    • Observation synchronisation. The A5H003 card showed a current forecast but a Latest Obs date of 30 March 2002. Treat GloFAS output as a forecast to be verified against national gauges, not as an observation.
    • Seasonal layer access. Some seasonal layers returned tiling or overview errors during the test; if a layer does not render, note it rather than substitute an assumption.
    • Forecast verification. Linking DWS hydrometric data (HYDSTRA / TWMS / LIMIS workflows) to the portal would allow forecast-versus-observed checks at the priority gauges; this is part of the proposed pilot.
    • GloFAS thresholds are percentiles of the station's own record, not surveyed flood levels (see Floods).
    • Gauge, station and site charts need a signed-in session.
