    Water accounting¶

    The question¶

    A basin planner or LIMCOM analyst needs a consistent balance of the basin's water: how much arrives as rain, how much leaves as evaporation and transpiration, how much runs off, how much is consumed by each sector, and how that balance is changing — the evidence base for allocation between the four riparian countries.

    The portal follows the Water Accounting Plus (WA+) framework: a resource-base sheet (inflows, storage change, landscape ET, utilisable outflow) with ET split by four land-use classes — Protected, Utilized, Modified and Managed.

    Who Decision Cadence
    LIMCOM secretariat and Technical Task Team Basin-wide allocation, transboundary reporting Annual accounts; hydrological-year review (Oct–Sep)
    National planners (DWS, DWA, ZINWA, DNGRH) Reconciliation strategies, licensing caps Annual
    Irrigation and hydropower sectors Efficiency targets, water-productivity benchmarks Seasonal
    Researchers and donors Track change and intervention impact Multi-year

    At a glance — WA+ accounts 2003–2024

    • Rainfall 265 km³/yr on average → ET 249 km³/yr (94 %) → outflow to the sea 6.8 km³/yr (2.6 %).
    • Range: rainfall from 151 km³ (2015) to 511 km³ (2021); outflow from 0.4 km³ (2005) to 52 km³ (2021).
    • 2024: rainfall 196 km³ against ET 278 km³ — the landscape drew 80 km³ from storage.
    • Land use: Utilized 76.6 % of area, Protected 15.6 %, Modified 5.9 %, Managed water use 2.0 % (8,038 km²).
    • Managed land use consumes ~8 % of basin ET — the only share that can be reallocated.

    Walkthrough¶

    1. Frame the balance. Explore data → add CHIRPS Historical Rainfall and Evaporation Africa. Set the timeline to the same month on both. Rain in, evaporation out: where evaporation exceeds rainfall the landscape is drawing down storage (Figure 1 shows this at basin scale for 2024).
    2. Split consumption. Replace Evaporation with Blue ET Africa and Green ET Africa, using Compare side by side. Blue ET is water that had to be supplied — the managed part of the balance; Green ET is rain used in place.
    3. Judge productivity. Add Beneficial Fraction Africa. High consumption with a low beneficial fraction is water lost without benefit — the first place to look for savings.
    4. Follow the runoff. Add SWAT Incremental Contributions and Mean Annual Runoff (LIMIS): which sub-catchments generate the river, and how does modelled contribution compare with the long-term mean. Add Water Yield by Zone (LIMIS) and Water Requirements by Zone (LIMIS) to see where demand exceeds yield.
    5. Attribute sectors. Add Irrigation Areas (LIMIS) and Hydropower Evaporation Footprint (Africa); for a scheme-level number, run Water Use Time-series (Irrigated Areas) (Polygon) over the scheme boundary.
    6. Account for transfers and storage. Add Inter-basin Water Transfers (LIMIS), Transmission Losses (LIMIS) and Dams & Reservoirs Basin Level Estimates — water that moves across the boundary, is lost in transit or sits in storage closes the balance.
    7. Check against stations. Add Water Balance Stations (LIMIS) and Evaporation Stations (LIMIS); click a station for observed values to validate the satellite terms.
    8. Open the WA+ Dashboard. Add Limpopo Basin Water Balance (WA+ Dashboard) and click the basin marker: the compiled accounts open as a Sankey (resource-base sheet) with a downloadable CSV of every term by month since 2003.
    9. Save the assembled view as a Story, one scene per term of the balance.

    Reading the charts¶

    The figures below are the portal's own charts, captured from the Feature Information panel (click a feature) and from the Charts dock that opens with the panel's Expand button. Every station, dam and site chart in the portal follows the same pattern: a summary card, a preview chart, Expand to enlarge, Download for the CSV.

    • This single marker is the entry point to the accounts: Balance Dashboard for the sheets, Download for the monthly CSV of every term since 2003.
    • The dock plots all terms at once, which is only useful for spotting the seasonal rhythm: the pink rainfall inflow peaks each summer; the yellow/orange ET terms follow one to two months later.
    • Turn series off in the legend to isolate a term — e.g. keep Inflow Precipitation Rainfall and Outflow ET Landscape Utilized to see that in dry summers ET exceeds rain.
    • For analysis use Download (one column per term) or the Balance Dashboard; the collapsed section below shows what the annual totals look like.
    • Observed discharge at the basin's gauges is the outflow term measured, not modelled; where it disagrees with the WA+ outflow, the account needs adjusting.

    ET / rainfall ratio (year) WA+ Dashboard; Figure 1 > 100 % Storage drawdown year — expect hydrological drought next season

    Blue ET concentration Blue ET Africa Hotspots outside registered Irrigation Areas (LIMIS) Unlicensed use — inspect
    Beneficial Fraction Beneficial Fraction Africa Low over irrigated blocks Efficiency programme target

    Worked example — explaining the 2024 account¶

    1. Open the WA+ Dashboard and select the 2024 hydrological year: rainfall 196 km³, ET 278 km³, outflow 1.2 km³.
    2. Add CHIRPS Historical Rainfall for Jan–Mar 2024 and Evaporation Africa for the same months with Compare: the ET map is still green over the eastern lowveld and the irrigated Olifants while rainfall is patchy — ET was sustained by stored water.
    3. Add Blue ET Africa: bright over Loskop, the Groot Letaba schemes and Chókwè — the managed share rose because irrigation continued through the deficit (Figure 2 shows the same effect in 2017).
    4. Add Dams & Reservoirs Basin Level Estimates for late 2024: storage drawdown through the year is the balancing term (Middle Letaba's decline begins here — see Reservoir monitoring).
    5. Add Water Requirements by Zone (LIMIS) and Water Yield by Zone (LIMIS): the zones where requirement exceeds yield are exactly where the 2024 deficit bit. Capture as a Story — the account, explained in five maps.

    Annual-account checklist¶

    • Hydrological year and data vintage of each term stated
    • Rainfall, ET and outflow (km³) with ET/rainfall and outflow/rainfall ratios
    • ET by land-use class, and the managed (blue) share
    • Storage change (reservoirs) reconciled against the residual
    • Sectoral consumption: irrigation (by scheme where possible), hydropower evaporation, urban
    • Transfers in / out and transmission losses
    • Validation against Water Balance / Evaporation Stations
    • Story link to the assembled map

    Limits to keep in mind¶

    • The Africa-wide ET products are model outputs at continental resolution; they are consistent across the basin (which is what accounting needs) but should be calibrated against stations before being quoted as volumes.
    • The WA+ accounts run 2003–2024; the current year appears only when the hydrological year closes.
    • Mean Annual Runoff (LIMIS) is a long-term average, not a current value.
    • Groundwater abstraction is only partly visible (through Blue ET and the groundwater layers); a full account needs borehole data.
    • The dashboard, the CSV download and the polygon time-series process require a signed-in session.
